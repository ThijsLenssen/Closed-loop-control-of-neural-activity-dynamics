\textbf{Carrasco's search in the plant-side dual formulation.}

Use R.2 with \(\underline\Theta=\mbf D(\Theta)\),
\(\underline{\mcl N}=-\mbf D(\mcl N)\), and
\(\mcl Z=\mcl P\mcl K^*\).
The scalar transfer multiplier \(M_{\mathrm{ZF}}\) below is distinct
from the full hard factor \(\Theta\).

\textbf{Enforce primal admissibility during the search.}
Set \(M_{\mathrm{ZF}}=1-H\) and
\(R_{\mathrm{ZF}}=M_{\mathrm{ZF}}^{-1}\).
Carrasco's norm constraints must enforce \(\|h\|_1<1\) for
\(H=1-R_{\mathrm{ZF}}^{-1}\), not for \(R_{\mathrm{ZF}}-1\).
For an anticausal realization
\(H=C_h(sI-A_h)^{-1}B_h\), Lemma 4.1 uses
\(Y=Y^\top\prec0\), \(\mu>0\), and fixed \(\lambda>0\):
\(\begin{bmatrix}A_h^\top Y+YA_h-\lambda Y&YB_h\\
B_h^\top Y&-\mu\end{bmatrix}\prec0\) and
\(\begin{bmatrix}-\lambda Y&0&C_h^\top\\
0&1-\mu&0\\C_h&0&1\end{bmatrix}\succ0\).
An inverse induced-gain bound alone does not establish this condition.

\textbf{Remove the inverse from the scalar positivity condition.}
For a rational SISO negative-feedback dual plant \(G_d\), set
\(F=1+kG_d\).
The reciprocal-multiplier condition
\(\operatorname{Re}\{R_{\mathrm{ZF}}F^*\}>0\)
is equivalent to \(\operatorname{Re}\{M_{\mathrm{ZF}}F\}>0\):
multiply by \(|M_{\mathrm{ZF}}|^2\).
The star here denotes frequency-wise complex conjugation, not
the causal system dual \((\cdot)^\prime\).
This recovers Carrasco's positivity condition, so his multiplier
variables can optimize the dual reciprocal implicitly while imposing
the primal norm constraint.

\textbf{Restricted joint synthesis construction.}
For the rational benchmark \(\underline{\mcl T}=I\), assume
\(\underline{\mcl A}_K=\underline{\mcl A}_0+
\mcl K^*\mcl B_{\mathrm u}^*\), with Carrasco's \(B_g,C_g,D_g\) fixed.
Choose fixed \(0<\beta<1\) and impose
\(S_{11}=\mcl P\succ0\), \(P_{11}=\beta\mcl P\).
Define \(E=\mcl P\underline{\mcl A}_0+\mcl Z\mcl B_{\mathrm u}^*\).
In Carrasco's Eq.~(17), substitute
\(S_{11}A_g=E\), \(A_g^\top S_{11}=E^*\),
\(P_{11}A_g=\beta E\), and \(A_g^\top P_{11}=\beta E^*\).
In Eqs.~(18)--(19), substitute
\(-\lambda(P_{11}-S_{11})=\lambda(1-\beta)\mcl P\).

For fixed \(k,\lambda,\beta\), these are joint LMIs in
\(\mcl P,\mcl Z,A_u,B_u,C_u,\mu\).
Here \(A_u,B_u,C_u\) are Carrasco's multiplier-search variables,
distinct from the plant actuator \(\mcl B_{\mathrm u}\).
Recover \(\mcl K=\mcl Z^*\mcl P^{-1}\), reconstruct
\(M_{\mathrm{ZF}}\) using Carrasco's referenced recovery procedure,
and obtain \(R_{\mathrm{ZF}}=M_{\mathrm{ZF}}^{-1}\).
The proportional-storage restriction is an additional conservative
construction derived here; it is not stated in Carrasco's paper.

\textbf{Remaining proof.}
The above establishes an algebraic rational-SISO construction.
The exact transport-PIE extension, controller recovery, and R.2
finite-horizon hard-factor and inverse conditions remain to be proved.
The scalar frequency transformation alone does not establish them.
Carrasco's Proposition 4.3 adds a Popov term whose improper part also
needs treatment in the bounded-filter framework.

\textbf{Sigmoid.}
Use \(\widetilde z=z-z_\star\) and
\(\widetilde\Delta(\widetilde z)
=\Delta(z_\star+\widetilde z)-\Delta(z_\star)\).
Centring preserves the full nonlinearity and gives
\(\widetilde\Delta(0)=0\).
Carrasco's Propositions 4.2--4.3 assume oddness; a logistic centred
at an arbitrary equilibrium need not be odd.
The non-odd case additionally requires nonnegative-kernel constraints.

Source: Carrasco et al., \emph{LMI searches for anticausal and noncausal
rational Zames--Falb multipliers}, Systems \& Control Letters 70 (2014),
17--22: Lemma 4.1, Proposition 4.2, Eqs.~(12)--(19), Proposition 4.3.
