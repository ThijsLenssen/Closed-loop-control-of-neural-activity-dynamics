Literature Review Notes

Paper notes

Separate reviews are stored in Paper_Notes/, with an index in its README.md. Each answers the
three per-paper questions; the synthesis below draws on those reference papers. Plain-English
definitions are collected in the medical and neuroscience nomenclature.


General questions

What can or is measured by optogenetics or deep brain stimulation?

Neural activity is measured, and firing rates can be estimated from those recordings.
In the optogenetic experiments of Zaaimi et al., multicontact electrodes record both LFPs and
single-neuron spikes; Fig. 3 reports spike-rate responses during stimulation [10, PDF pp. 2, 5].
Electrical-stimulation experiments used by Nevado Holgado et al. likewise record STN/GPe
firing rates, including GPe responses to STN stimulation [15, PDF pp. 3–4]. The DBS review
by Neumann et al. summarizes measured single-neuron firing responses at several stimulation
targets [12, PDF pp. 4–6].
For a population represented by ni recorded neurons, a possible measurement construction is the
average spike count per unit time:

LFPs also provide a route to observing rate dynamics. Zaaimi et al. model LFP as a high-pass-
filtered combination of excitatory and inhibitory population activity [10, PDF pp. 9, 15]. Hall et
al., cited by that paper, demonstrate real-time estimation of individual cortical firing rates from
multichannel LFPs using a trained decoder [3]. Optical indicators offer another route to activity
estimation [1, 2]. Thus both spike-based rate estimates and calibrated indirect observations can
support a firing-rate state model. The observation map depends on the recording method; an
unprocessed LFP is not itself a firing rate.


Is state feedback sufficient, or is an observer needed?

State feedback is an explicit measurement assumption. It is experimentally plausible
when the states used by the controller are population rates estimated from representative,
separately identified recordings of those populations. Sampling error, rate filtering and latency
must then be included in the implementation model. Recording neural activity alone does not
establish access to every model state.
Evidence for estimation. Zaaimi et al. feed back one filtered LFP channel, while their model
contains both E and I [10, PDF pp. 2, 9, 14–15]. Their experiment demonstrates output feedback,
rather than separate measurement of both states. Hall et al. demonstrate an LFP-to-firing-rate
decoder [3]. More directly, Bolus et al. synthesize linear-quadratic state-feedback gains and

What can or is controlled by optogenetics or deep brain stimulation?

Stimulation supplies an input to neuronal populations and changes their firing
dynamics. Optogenetics targets opsin-expressing neurons or projections. Zaaimi et al. explicitly
represent optical drive S(t) as an input to the excitatory population and measure the resulting
spiking and LFP activity [10, PDF pp. 2, 5, 15]. The reviewed optogenetic perturbations of
STN/GPe also produce recorded changes in neuronal firing rates [14, PDF pp. 6–8].
For electrical stimulation, Nevado Holgado et al. model STN electrode current through an
additive input and fit its effect to GPe firing-rate responses [15, PDF pp. 3–4, Eq. 5]. DBS
recruits neurons and axons, thereby influencing the stimulated and connected populations [12].
These examples support both input locations in the proposed model: cu u changes the drive
entering a population’s nonlinear activation, whereas bu u represents an additive effective rate
drive. Targeting determines which channels are actuated; kinetics and pathway recruitment
determine their gains, signs and dynamics.


What causes the neurological symptoms?

For Parkinson’s disease, dopamine loss changes population firing rates, synaptic interactions and
the temporal organization of basal-ganglia activity. A dynamical firing-rate model can represent
both changes in mean activity and collective oscillations through time-varying population rates
[15, 16]. Exaggerated beta synchronization is associated particularly with bradykinesia and
rigidity; its contribution to symptoms must be distinguished from the mechanism generating it.
Motor deficits can precede exaggerated beta [14, 9].
The mechanism is not settled by a single loop: delayed STN–GPe models and models emphasizing
pallidostriatal interactions provide different accounts of synchronization [15, 16, 17]. For freezing
of gait, cortical PAC is associated with severity, yet DBS improves freezing even at comparable
PAC levels. Thus PAC reduction alone does not explain the treatment effect [5].

Implication for optimization
For fixed positive profiles aj (s), expand βj (s) in a finite polynomial basis and impose nonnegativity, for
example through an SOS certificate on [0, 1]. The weight coefficients and d are then candidate decision
variables. An SDP requires the complete IQC/storage inequality to be affine in all joint decision variables;
affinity of B, D alone does not establish this. Jointly varying aj (s) and a Lyapunov operator generally
introduces products of unknowns. Alternating between fixed-profile and fixed-Lyapunov searches is
therefore a possible heuristic when those subproblems are convex, with no guarantee of a global optimum.

Symptoms, disorders and treatment terms
Akinesia                    Difficulty initiating movement, or a marked lack of movement.

Bradykinesia                Slowness of movement, often with progressively smaller or slower
                            repeated movements.
Cognitive                   Relating to processes such as attention, memory, learning and
                            decision-making.

Dystonia                    Involuntary muscle contractions causing abnormal postures or repetitive
                            twisting movements.
Epilepsy / seizure          A seizure is an episode of abnormal brain activity; epilepsy is a disorder
                            involving an enduring tendency to have seizures.
Essential tremor            A movement disorder characterized mainly by tremor during posture or
                            action.
Freezing of gait / FoG      A brief inability or marked difficulty to step despite the intention to
                            walk.
Gait                        The pattern of walking.
Huntington’s disease        An inherited neurodegenerative disorder affecting movement, cognition
                            and behaviour.
Hypokinetic /               Hypokinetic refers to reduced movement; hyperkinetic refers to excessive

Levodopa / L-DOPA           A substance converted into dopamine in the body and used to treat

Motor / non-motor           Motor symptoms affect movement; non-motor symptoms include effects

Sleep and experimental terminology
Acute / chronic                 Acute usually refers to a short-term condition or experiment; chronic
                                refers to a persistent condition or long-term recording/intervention.
Anaesthetized                   Under anaesthesia; the drugs can affect neural activity and responses to
                                stimulation.
Brain slice                     A thin piece of brain tissue kept viable outside the animal for
                                experiments.
Biophysical                     Describing biological activity through physical mechanisms such as
                                membrane currents and ion channels.
Causal / correlational          Causal evidence supports one process influencing another; correlation
evidence                        describes variables changing together.
Histology                       Examining tissue structure, usually with microscopy and stains.
Epidural                        Located outside the dura mater, the tough outer membrane surrounding
                                the brain and spinal cord.
In silico / in vitro / in       In silico: in a computer model. In vitro: outside the living organism. In
vivo                            vivo: within a living organism.
NHP                             Non-human primate, such as a monkey used in research.
NREM sleep / N1–N3              Non-rapid-eye-movement sleep, subdivided into stages N1, N2 and N3.
REM sleep                       Rapid-eye-movement sleep: a sleep state with characteristic brain
                                activity, eye movements and usually reduced muscle tone.
Sleep decoding                  Estimating sleep state from recorded signals using an algorithm.
Transgenic animal               An animal whose genetic material has been deliberately altered, for
                                example to express an opsin in selected neurons.
6-OHDA / MPTP model             Experimental Parkinsonian models using substances that damage
                                dopamine systems; their effects do not reproduce every feature of
                                human disease.
